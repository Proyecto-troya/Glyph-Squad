The problem, in one sentence

Because of this tool, Noor will send her cooperative's technician a count of which leaf problem shows on how many of 30 coffee leaves, the same weekend she notices spots, a report she would otherwise make only when he visits, which was twice last year; we know because only 3.1% of Peruvian producers received technical assistance in 2024 (ENA). What we do not know yet: our model was right on 88.1% of Brazilian test leaves and on only 46.7% of leaves from San Martín, Peru.

Our own tests: BRACOL test split, 202 leaves, five classes; Saposoa, 999 photos, healthy and rust only. The model saw no Peruvian leaf in training.

Why AI, and where it stops

On the phone, AI has one job. The rest is rules and people.

THE ONE JOB: TELL FIVE LEAF CONDITIONS APART, OR ADMIT DOUBT

An SMS, a spreadsheet or a search cannot look at a leaf, and a picture card leaves the naming to her. What an SMS can do, we leave to SMS.

NOT AI, ON PURPOSE

  • The photo-quality check
  • Counting the leaves
  • The plant-age question
  • The fixed messages and audio
  • The SMS code and the technician's list

A SECOND, OPTIONAL AI, OFF THE PHONE

With a connection, a small language model writes one sentence for the technician. It sees only the plot code and the counts, and a server rejects any sentence whose numbers do not match.

Human in the loopShe presses send. The technician decides.
No hallucinationsWhat Noor reads and hears is a fixed list. The one generated sentence is checked against the counts, or dropped.
Fail-safeLow confidence or a bad photo: “Not sure, ask the technician.”
Privacy and limitsPhotos stay on the phone; only the plot code and counts leave it. No doses, no promises.
Leaf drawings are schematic. The language model is llama3.2:3b on the team's laptop, or a hosted Llama 3.1 8B on the deployed site. The app works without either.07 / 10

Data and evidence

Right in Brazil, wrong in Peru. We measured both.

TRAINSBRACOLBrazil, 2019 · 1,747 arabica leaves, underside up on white. We trained on 1,401: the published zip is truncatedCC BY 4.0
TESTS, NEVER SEENSaposoaPeru (San Martín), 2026 · 999 healthy and rust photos, 500 of ojo de gallo, a disease the model never sawCC BY 4.0
WHAT THE DATA MISSESNo leaves from Cusco, and no photos taken by farmers on a low-cost Android. The Peruvian set tests only healthy and rust. No ojo de gallo or nutrient deficiencies in training. The Quechua is machine-translated, synthetic, non-commercial and not validated.

The refusal threshold was tuned on Brazilian leaves, where it answers 89.5% of photos at 90% accuracy. It does not hold in Peru.
